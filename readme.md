# Radar Interference Countermeasure Breakthrough at Low Signal-to-Noise Ratio: How CVEDNet Precisely "Filters" False Targets? [with Python code]

Interrupted Sampling Forward Interference (ISRJ) poses a significant challenge to radar systems by generating false targets in the range direction. Current anti-interference methods struggle to suppress ISRJ while preserving the integrity of real target signals at low signal-to-noise ratio (SNR), leading to degradation in sidelobes and reduced detection performance. This paper introduces a complex value encoder-decoder network (CVEDNet) based on signal decomposition for end-to-end ISRJ suppression: directly processing complex valued time-domain signals without the need for additional preprocessing. The encoder-decoder structure progressively compresses and reconstructs the noise, extracting more compact echo features; employing stacked dual-branch structures and multi-scale expansion convolutions to further separate the echo from ISRJ based on high-dimensional features; designing a multi-domain joint loss function that integrates waveform and range pulse compression information to ensure the reconstruction of echo amplitude and phase completeness during training. Experiments show that this method outperforms traditional methods in terms of interference resistance, echo fidelity, and detection performance under low SNR conditions.
Radar technology utilizes electromagnetic waves to detect targets, playing an important role in military, aerospace, traffic monitoring, meteorological observation, and remote sensing [1-4]. The rapid development of radar technology brings new possibilities to both military and civilian sectors but also introduces new challenges. In military applications, the complex electromagnetic environment in electronic countermeasures significantly affects radar detection performance [5,6].
Interrupted Sampling Forward Interference (ISRJ) operates through a storage-forward mechanism to interfere [7,8]. It generates multiple false targets on the range axis, leading to tracking errors or even failures. As a coherent interference technique, ISRJ leverages coherent pulse compression to achieve processing gain while reducing transmit power. Typically, ISRJ interferers operate within the duration of a single radar pulse, rendering pulse-to-pulse diversity defense measures ineffective [9,10]. Moreover, by adjusting interference parameters flexibly, different interference effects can be achieved, altering the distribution of false targets.
Researchers have proposed methods to reduce the coherence of ISRJ signals by enhancing waveform diversity or modifying the filter kernel functions. For example, authors in [11] introduced pulse-in-pulse dual-parameter Variable Angular Frequency (VAFR) waveforms to enhance the discriminability between radar signals and ISRJ signals, followed by parallel suppression techniques based on fractional Fourier transform. Similarly, authors in [12] designed mismatch filters specifically for interference signals to reduce the peak level of false targets, and optimized using an improved alternating projection algorithm. However, for ISRJ with variable interference parameters, this method requires recalibrating the mismatch filters based on the incident signal, which leads to a decrease in real-time countermeasures. Moreover, the mismatch filter kernel reduces the coherence between the filter kernel and the true target signal, resulting in energy loss in the range image.
Beyond waveform and matched filter design, reconstructing and de-interference methods have also become a focal point in the field. As time progresses, various parameter estimation algorithms have been developed to reconstruct ISRJ signals. In [13], Zhou Chao utilized the time-frequency analysis of pulse compression results to determine the number of slices and forwarding intervals, followed by an inverse convolution to estimate the slice width and then reconstructed and de-interfered interference signals in the distance domain. However, due to the approximation of the principle of stationary phase in this method, the mathematical model for interference slices introduces errors inevitably leading to residual interference signals. In [14], a method combining time-domain inverse convolution with box-shaped edge estimation differences was proposed to improve the accuracy of interference edge localization estimates and minimize interference residuals in the time domain. In [15], by searching within the truncation matched filter matrix, the delay and distribution of interference slices were determined using the minimum residual energy criterion. Additionally, in [16], a one-dimensional semi-parametric technique was employed to separate target echoes from received interfered signals, followed by an improved k-means clustering algorithm to distinguish between interference signals and target echoes, thereby effectively reconstructing the interfered signals. Although these methods are well-designed and introduce some innovative concepts into the field of signal processing, the ultimate effectiveness of interference combat largely depends on the accuracy of parameter estimation, which is difficult to ensure under low Signal-to-Noise Ratio (SNR) conditions.
In recent years, leveraging the powerful feature extraction capabilities of deep learning, several interference suppression schemes based on deep learning have emerged. The authors of [17,18] utilized autoencoders, bidirectional Gated Recurrent Units (BiGRU), and Gated Recurrent Units (GRU) among other neural networks to create masks for undisturbed signals, retaining these signal segments, and then reconstructed complete target echoes using compressive sensing. Similarly, the authors of [19,20] applied the YOLO algorithm to identify false and true targets in time-frequency images, extracting both their temporal-distance and frequency domain information. [21] utilized the UT-net to preliminarily suppress interference in time-frequency spectra, followed by ResCU-net to repair the signal loss caused by time-frequency transformation. Compared to traditional methods, deep learning demonstrates stronger intelligent fitting capability and higher accuracy under strong interference and low signal-to-noise ratio scenarios. However, the interference suppression methods mentioned above require at least two stages: parameter estimation and interference cancellation. Moreover, the potential of deep learning networks still awaits further exploration.
When suppressing ISRJ under different signal-to-noise ratio conditions, maintaining the amplitude and phase of the target echo is crucial. This paper proposes an end-to-end time-domain complex value network for ISRJ suppression, namely the Complex Valued Codec Network (CVEDNet). This end-to-end network does not require any preprocessing, takes input with interfered signals, and outputs a clean echo signal, both inputs and outputs being in the time-domain complex value form. The network adopts a codec architecture, designing stacked dual branch expansion convolution structures in the feature selection layer to separate ISRJ from the echo based on compact signal features. At the same time, a multi-domain joint loss function is designed to suppress ISRJ while reconstructing the target signal, ensuring high fidelity.
The core idea of the article is based on the "signal separation" concept, which achieves end-to-end processing through a complex value decoding structure: inputting complex value time-domain signals containing ISRJ, outputting reconstructed pure target echoes. The key lies in noise suppression and feature separation - firstly, noise is compressed and filtered through encoding, then high-dimensional features are used to separate real echoes from ISRJ, finally, the signal is decoded and reconstructed.
Network architecture
Encoder
Feature extraction using two scales (4 and 16) of complex convolutional kernels in parallel, followed by max pooling to align dimensions, enhances feature representation capabilities while suppressing noise.
Feature selection module
The architecture consists of a bottleneck layer, a stacked dual-branch convolutional block (DBCB), and an expansion layer. The DBCB includes a feature fusion and extraction branch, which utilizes different expansion rates (2^0).
Up to \(2^7\)
The expansion convolution captures multi-scale features, gradually separating ISRJ from the true echo.
Decoder
Through back-convolution, the high-dimensional features are restored to a complex value time-domain signal, ensuring that the output is consistent with the input signal dimensionality and retains key phase and amplitude information.
The multi-domain joint loss function integrates the temporal mean square error (MSE) and pulse compression domain perception loss, while simultaneously constraining waveform similarity and signal coherence. This approach avoids the limitations of a single loss function, enhancing the quality of reconstructed signals.

The performance is significantly better than traditional methods such as MaxTF and BCS, with only a 0.25dB drop in the peak sidelobe ratio (PSLR), and excellent phase fidelity.
Strong robustness
Faced with different interference duty cycle and interference signal ratio (JSR), the scale-invariant signal-to-noise ratio (SISNR) of reconstructed signals remains stable, even when the interference intensity increases to 50dB, it can still effectively suppress false targets.
Good generalization
The untrained frequency modulation ISRJ (false target ahead of real target) can still be effectively suppressed, with only slight degradation in reconstruction quality.
CVEDNet provides an end-to-end solution for radar anti-ISRRJ, which can be expanded to multi-target scenarios and compound interference suppression in the future, further enhancing the adaptability of radar in complex environments.
